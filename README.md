# App Description and Purpose

## Overview

This app is a mobile farming education and farm-management companion designed for iOS and Android. Its purpose is to guide users through the complete process of farming a crop in a structured, practical, and easy-to-follow way.

The first version focuses on **pepper farming**. Instead of giving users a collection of articles or unrelated farming videos, the app takes them through a complete pepper farming journey, beginning with preparation and ending with harvest, selling, and estimated profit review.

The central experience is:

> **Learn → Watch → Practice → Complete → Track → Grow → Harvest → Sell → Review Profit**

The app is designed to make users feel as if they are managing a real farming project—not simply taking an online course.

***

## The Problem We Are Solving

Many people interested in farming face several challenges:

- They do not know where to start.
- Farming information is often scattered across videos, articles, and social media.
- Generic videos may not explain what the learner should do next.
- Users may watch educational content without actually practicing it.
- There is often no system for tracking progress.
- Beginners may not know which materials, tools, or activities are required at each stage.
- Farmers may complete the farming process without properly recording expenses, harvest quantities, sales, or profit.
- Users may have questions but no immediate source of guidance connected to the lesson they are following.

YouTube and other platforms provide access to farming content, but they generally do not guide users through a complete, connected farming journey. A user may watch a video about planting peppers, but still not know what to prepare first, what to do next, how to test their understanding, or how to record the financial outcome.

This app is intended to solve that problem by bringing learning, practical activities, progress tracking, farm records, and financial review into one mobile experience.

***

## What the App Is All About

The app is a **structured farming journey platform**.

A user does not simply choose a video and watch it. Instead, the user starts a farming program, such as Pepper Farming, and progresses through a sequence of stages and activities.

For example, the journey may include:

1. Preparing the farming area.
2. Preparing seeds or seedlings.
3. Planting.
4. Monitoring early growth.
5. Managing weeds and plant nutrition.
6. Protecting the crop.
7. Following flowering and fruit development.
8. Harvesting.
9. Preparing produce for sale.
10. Recording sales.
11. Reviewing expenses and estimated profit.

Each stage contains practical learning activities. The actual number of activities and the timing of each stage should be based on the client’s real farming process. The app should remain flexible instead of forcing every crop into an artificial fixed number of days.

The system is therefore based on:

> **Stages + Activities + Timing**

***

## The Core User Experience

When a user opens the app, they should immediately understand their current position in the farming journey.

The Home Dashboard may show:

- The current program: Pepper Farming.
- The user’s progress percentage.
- The current farming day or activity.
- The current stage.
- Today’s task.
- The upcoming task.
- A button to continue the lesson.
- A quick way to ask the Farming AI Assistant.
- Recent journal activity.

For example:

> **Your Pepper Farm**  
> Day 18 of your farming journey  
> Current Stage: Crop Maintenance  
> Today’s Task: Weed Management

The user selects **Continue Today’s Lesson** and enters a guided learning experience.

That lesson may include:

- The objective for the day.
- The tools and materials required.
- A step-by-step procedure.
- Images showing what to look for.
- A short video recorded on the real farm.
- An interactive practice activity.
- A knowledge check.
- Contextual help from the AI assistant.
- A completion action.

After finishing the activity, the user marks it complete. The app then updates their progress and makes the next activity available.

***

## Practical Learning, Not Passive Watching

The app is deliberately designed to be more than a video platform.

Every lesson should combine several forms of learning:

- Simple written explanations.
- Real farm photos.
- Short practical videos.
- Interactive questions.
- Step-ordering activities.
- Scenario-based questions.
- Image identification.
- Knowledge checks.
- Completion tracking.
- AI explanations and quizzes.

For example, after watching a video about preparing the planting area, the app may ask:

> “You have prepared the planting area. What should happen next?”

The user selects an answer and receives an explanation. If the answer is incorrect, the app explains the recommended process instead of simply displaying “Wrong.”

This makes the app educational and practical. It encourages the user to understand the reason behind an activity, not merely copy instructions.

***

## Real Farm Demonstrations

The app should use original content recorded by the client on their own farm.

Instead of relying primarily on generic online videos, the client can record demonstrations showing:

- How to select and prepare the farming area.
- How to prepare the soil.
- How to prepare seedlings.
- How to plant pepper.
- How to apply proper spacing.
- How to water after planting.
- How to manage weeds.
- How to support crop growth.
- How to protect the crop.
- How to identify harvest readiness.
- How to handle and prepare produce for sale.

Long videos should be divided into short, focused sections. For example, a planting lesson could include separate videos for:

- Preparing the soil.
- Preparing seedlings.
- Planting.
- Spacing.
- Initial watering.

This makes the content easier to follow and allows users to focus on one practical task at a time.

***

## The Farming AI Assistant

The app includes an AI Farming Assistant, but it should not feel like a completely separate chatbot.

The AI should understand the user’s current context, including:

- The crop being studied.
- The current farming stage.
- The current activity.
- The lesson content.
- The procedure being explained.
- The user’s progress.

A user should be able to ask:

- “Explain step three in simpler terms.”
- “Why is this step important?”
- “What do I need for today’s activity?”
- “Can you test me on this lesson?”
- “What did I learn today?”
- “I do not understand this part.”
- “What should I do next?”

The AI’s primary responsibility is to explain, simplify, clarify, and quiz the user based on the client’s documented farming procedures.

The client’s farming knowledge remains the source of truth. The AI should not invent instructions or provide recommendations that contradict the course content. Its role is to support the learning experience, not replace the client’s expertise.

***

## Tracking the Real Farming Journey

The app should track both educational progress and real farming activity.

Educational tracking may include:

- Lessons completed.
- Activities completed.
- Practice scores.
- Current stage.
- Upcoming activities.
- Completion percentage.
- Milestones and badges.

Real farming tracking may include:

- What the user did on a particular date.
- Notes about farm activities.
- Quantities used or produced.
- Farm photographs.
- Harvest records.
- Sales records.
- Expenses.

The user should have a digital farm journal where they can record activities such as:

- Land preparation.
- Planting.
- Watering.
- Weeding.
- Fertilizing.
- Crop protection.
- Harvesting.
- Selling.

A journal entry may contain:

- Date.
- Activity.
- Notes.
- Quantity.
- Unit.
- Optional photograph.

For example:

> September 29 — Watering — Completed today’s activity.

Users can also upload photographs of their own farm and compare crop progress over time.

This gives the app value beyond education. It becomes a practical record of the user’s farming project.

***

## Harvest, Selling, and Financial Review

The journey should not end when the user finishes watching the final lesson. It should continue through the real outcome of farming.

At the harvest stage, the user should be able to learn:

- How to know the crop is ready.
- How to harvest correctly.
- What to consider during harvesting.
- How to handle the produce.
- How to prepare it for sale.

The user can then record:

- Harvest date.
- Harvest quantity.
- Unit of measurement.
- Crop condition.
- Notes.
- Optional photo.

At the selling stage, the user can record:

- Date of sale.
- Quantity sold.
- Selling price.
- Total revenue.
- Buyer or market details, if needed.
- Notes.

The app should also allow users to record expenses such as:

- Seeds or seedlings.
- Land preparation.
- Labor.
- Fertilizer.
- Crop protection.
- Watering.
- Transport.
- Packaging.
- Other costs.

The Profit Summary then displays:

- Total expenses.
- Total sales.
- Estimated profit.
- Total harvest quantity.
- Total quantity sold.
- Remaining quantity, if applicable.

The basic calculation is:

\[
\text{Estimated Profit} = \text{Total Sales} - \text{Total Expenses}
\]

The result should be shown in FCFA by default.

The app must clearly state that this is an estimate based on the user’s entries and is not a guarantee of earnings.

***

## Why Pepper Is the First Crop

The first version focuses on one crop: pepper.

This is intentional. Starting with one complete farming program makes it easier to:

- Produce high-quality lessons.
- Record relevant demonstrations.
- Test the user experience.
- Validate the learning journey.
- Avoid overwhelming users.
- Build and test the product faster.
- Create a reusable structure for future crops.

The goal is not to launch with pepper, yam, tomatoes, corn, cassava, plantain, and vegetables all at once. The goal is to create one well-designed and complete pepper farming experience.

Once the pepper program is working well, the same structure can be reused for other crops.

Future programs may include:

- Yam farming.
- Tomato farming.
- Corn farming.
- Cassava farming.
- Plantain farming.
- Vegetable farming.

Each new crop can use the same underlying structure of stages, activities, content, timing, progress, records, and outcomes.

## What Makes the App Different

The app’s value comes from combining several features in one experience.

| Capability | Value to the user |
|---|---|
| Structured learning | The user knows exactly what to learn and do next. |
| Real farm demonstrations | The user sees the client’s actual farming process. |
| Step-by-step activities | The user receives practical instructions instead of general information. |
| Interactive practice | The user checks whether they understood the lesson. |
| Contextual AI assistance | The user can ask questions about the exact lesson they are following. |
| Progress tracking | The app remembers the user’s position in the journey. |
| Farm journal | The user records what happened on their own farm. |
| Farm photos | The user documents and compares crop progress. |
| Harvest records | The user records actual production results. |
| Sales records | The user records quantities and income. |
| Expense records | The user can calculate a more meaningful result. |
| Profit review | The user sees an estimated financial outcome. |

The app is not trying to compete with YouTube as a video platform. It is offering what a normal video platform does not provide: structure, progression, practice, context, records, and completion.

***

## What We Are Trying to Accomplish

The main objective is to help users move from **interest in farming** to **structured practical action**.

We want users to be able to:

- Start a farming program with confidence.
- Understand what to do at every stage.
- Learn from the client’s real farming experience.
- Watch practical demonstrations.
- Practice what they have learned.
- Ask questions when they are confused.
- Track educational progress.
- Record activities on their own farm.
- Document crop development.
- Record harvest quantities.
- Record sales and expenses.
- Review an estimated financial result.
- Complete a full farming journey rather than consume disconnected content.

The desired emotional outcome is:

> “I am not just watching someone farm. I am following a farming journey myself.”

The app should make farming feel more understandable, organized, and achievable for beginners and developing farmers.

***

## The MVP Goal

The first version should answer one important question:

> Can the app take a user through a complete pepper farming learning journey in an engaging and practical way?

The Minimum Viable Product should focus on:

- Account creation and login.
- Pepper Farming as the first program.
- Home dashboard.
- Farming journey timeline.
- Structured stages and activities.
- Daily lessons.
- Step-by-step instructions.
- Original farm videos.
- Interactive practice.
- Knowledge checks.
- Contextual AI assistance.
- Progress tracking.
- Farm journal.
- Farm photo uploads.
- Harvest tracking.
- Sales recording.
- Expense recording.
- Basic profit calculation.
- Profile and settings.

The first version should not be overloaded with features such as community forums, advanced weather tools, multiple crops, complex market data, certificates, or advanced farm-management systems.

Those features can be introduced after the core farming journey has been tested and validated.

***

## Long-Term Vision

The long-term vision is to develop a broader digital farming platform where users can manage multiple farming programs and projects from one mobile app.

Future possibilities include:

- Multiple crop programs.
- Multiple farming projects.
- Crop calendars.
- Weather information.
- Farming calculators.
- Personalized learning paths.
- Advanced AI support.
- Expert consultations.
- Community discussions.
- Market information.
- Advanced financial tracking.
- Farmer profiles.
- Farm-management tools.
- Certificates for completed programs.
- More advanced assessments.

However, the foundation remains the same:

> Guide the user from learning to practical action, from practical action to farming records, and from farming records to measurable outcomes.

***

## Final Product Definition

This app is a mobile farming companion that combines education, practical training, farm journaling, crop progress tracking, harvest records, sales records, and financial review.

It begins with pepper farming and guides the user through a flexible, step-by-step journey based on real agricultural processes.

The app is intended to transform farming education from passive content consumption into an active experience:

> **The user learns what to do, watches how it is done, practices the process, completes the activity, records the real-world result, and continues until harvest, selling, and profit review.**

That is what the app is all about, and that is what we are trying to accomplish.
